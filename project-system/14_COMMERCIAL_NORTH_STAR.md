# 14 — Commercial North Star

Status: working commercial strategy. Revisit after alpha evidence, first affiliate conversion, and first B2B pilot.

## Why this document exists

Scoop can be copied at the surface level. A basic flow such as:

`capture frame -> call vision model -> show product links`

is not defensible and should not be treated as the business.

The company must become valuable because it owns and continuously improves the harder layer:

`visual intent -> evidence -> canonical product identity -> verification -> merchant availability -> commercial outcome`

The extension is distribution. The product-resolution system, merchant/catalog network, outcome data, and visual-intent graph are the long-term asset.

## Commercial north star

Build Scoop into the neutral visual-commerce infrastructure that converts a deliberate visual gesture into a verified product-intent event and routes that intent to useful purchasable products without allowing commercial incentives to corrupt identity.

Scoop should eventually make money from three directions around the same core engine:

1. businesses paying to use the infrastructure,
2. developers paying to build on the infrastructure,
3. merchants paying for commerce outcomes generated by the infrastructure.

Consumer usage supplies intent and distribution. It is not required to be the sole source of revenue.

## Near-term objective

Reach meaningful recurring revenue without requiring mass consumer scale.

The first commercial milestone is not "millions of users." It is:

- a working capped alpha,
- measured cost per completed Scoop,
- at least one tracked affiliate conversion,
- at least one paying B2B customer or pilot,
- evidence that users repeat the Scoop interaction.

## $10k MRR strategy

Affiliate revenue alone is unlikely to be the fastest path to $10k MRR because it requires substantial transaction volume while Scoop still bears inference and infrastructure cost.

The preferred revenue mix is:

### 1. B2B visual-commerce infrastructure

Sell an SDK/API or white-label integration to retailers, publishers, video platforms, commerce companies, or catalog owners.

Customer value:
- avoid building and maintaining visual-product resolution internally,
- make video or visual content commercially actionable,
- resolve against first-party inventory where available,
- fall back to external merchant/catalog sources where permitted,
- receive confidence-calibrated EXACT / LIKELY / SIMILAR results,
- gain analytics on what viewers actually select.

Possible commercial structures:
- fixed monthly platform fee,
- usage-based pricing,
- fixed-fee pilot,
- hybrid subscription + usage,
- subscription + commerce revenue share.

Working revenue target examples, not pricing commitments:
- 10 customers x $1,000/month = $10k MRR,
- 5 customers x $2,000/month = $10k MRR,
- 4 customers x $2,500/month = $10k MRR.

The initial pitch should be build-vs-buy economics and speed, not claims of proprietary magic AI.

### 2. Scoop Developer / BYOK

Offer developers the Scoop product-resolution and commerce stack while allowing them to bring supported model-provider keys.

Scoop supplies:
- visual intent schema,
- evidence normalization,
- candidate retrieval,
- verification,
- canonical product resolution,
- confidence classes,
- merchant routing,
- commerce attribution,
- analytics/debugging appropriate to the tier.

The developer supplies some or all model inference capacity through BYOK.

Benefits:
- caps Scoop's inference liability,
- supports high-volume technical users,
- reinforces provider independence,
- creates high-margin recurring revenue if developers value the commerce layer.

Do not sell "access to Gemini/OpenAI." Sell the verified visual-commerce pipeline.

### 3. Performance commerce

Use affiliate networks, direct merchant programs, or compliant CPC/CPA arrangements to monetize qualified purchase intent.

Flow:

`viewer selects object -> Scoop resolves product -> merchant destination -> tracked click/sale -> Scoop earns`

Use this first as:
- proof that Scoop generates commercial value,
- supplemental revenue,
- merchant-distribution infrastructure,
- a source of conversion/outcome data.

Do not assume affiliate revenue alone will fund the company at small scale.

## Affiliate-network strategy

### Awin — now

Join as a publisher/affiliate partner first.

Purpose:
- access advertisers and product feeds,
- create tracked merchant destinations,
- validate the first complete commercial loop,
- learn which categories and merchants convert.

Awin publisher promotional profile should accurately describe Scoop as a visual product-discovery/comparison experience rather than a traditional content publisher.

Potential future path: Awin Technology Partner.

Awin's Technology Partner program can support technology distributed through its MasterTag ecosystem. Technology partners are pre-vetted and may use CPA, fixed-fee, or hybrid commercial models. This is a future distribution opportunity after Scoop has evidence of value. Do not pay integration fees or pursue deep MasterTag work before product evidence justifies it.

### Sovrn Commerce — next monetization adapter to evaluate

Purpose:

### Skimlinks — later / optional

Purpose:

### Impact / direct networks — later

Use when specific merchants, better economics, stronger feeds, or direct commercial relationships justify the additional integration work.

## Merchant relationship model

Do not begin by asking every brand to pay a SaaS fee.

A merchant can enter Scoop in stages:

### Stage 1 — passive merchant destination

Scoop routes qualified traffic through an existing compliant affiliate/CPC relationship.

### Stage 2 — catalog/feed partner

Merchant supplies richer product data:
- SKU/product ID,
- title,
- brand/model,
- product images / alternate angles,
- category,
- attributes,
- price,
- availability,
- geography,
- merchant destination.

Better merchant data can improve candidate generation and exact-match evidence.

### Stage 3 — strategic commerce partner

Merchant pays via:
- fixed fee,
- usage,
- CPA/CPC,
- hybrid arrangement,
- premium catalog/inventory integration.

Commercial terms never change product identity or relevance thresholds.

## Platform / publisher relationship model

A publisher or platform is commercially interesting when it has one or more of:
- substantial video or visual traffic,
- a product catalog,
- creator/product metadata,
- affiliate relationships,
- a need for visual-intent analytics,
- enough engineering opportunity cost to prefer buying rather than building.

A company with one lightly used marketing video and its own catalog is usually a weak SaaS prospect. Its larger need may be traffic acquisition, not visual-commerce software.

Stronger targets:
- retailers with large video/content libraries,
- publishers with affiliate-commerce operations,
- video platforms serving multiple publishers,
- video CMS/player companies,
- marketplaces/catalog owners,
- retailers wanting visual search against large first-party catalogs.

## Exact-match strategy

Never promise that every visual selection will produce an exact match.

Exact identity should improve through evidence accumulation:

`selected pixels`
`+ neighboring frames when permitted`
`+ logos/text/marks`
`+ video/context metadata`
`+ first-party creator/publisher metadata`
`+ partner inventory/catalog`
`+ candidate images`
`+ historical product mappings`
`= calibrated identity decision`

Return:
- EXACT when evidence strongly supports the same product,
- LIKELY when meaningful uncertainty remains,
- SIMILAR when commercially useful but not represented as the original.

First-party inventories and platform metadata are evidence sources, not automatic truth. Contradictions still go through the normal verification pipeline.

Affiliate networks must sit behind a Scoop-owned abstraction. Do not spread Awin-, Sovrn-, or other network-specific response shapes through product logic.

Suggested future abstraction:

```ts
interface AffiliateResolver {
  resolve(input: {
    merchant: string
    destination: string
    productId?: string
    geography?: string
  }): Promise<CommercialDestination | null>

```

Commerce monetization happens only after identity/relevance gating.

## What becomes defensible

Today the visible feature is easy to recreate.

The moat must compound from assets that are slower and more expensive to reproduce:

1. visual-intent graph,
2. canonical product graph across merchants,
3. exact/likely/similar evidence history,
4. product-image and metadata normalization across providers,
5. user corrections and confirmed matches,
6. conversion/outcome data,
7. first-party retailer/publisher catalog integrations,
8. merchant/affiliate routing relationships,
9. category/provider performance data,
10. benchmark and regression corpus,
11. multi-frame identity evidence,
12. trusted neutrality between identity and monetization.

The commercial objective is to make "build it ourselves" progressively less attractive because reproducing the full system requires rebuilding years of integrations, normalized data, evaluation evidence, outcome history, and operational reliability.

## Data flywheel

```text
more users / partner traffic
        -> more deliberate visual-intent events
        -> more candidate comparisons
        -> more corrections and confirmed identities
        -> more merchant clicks/conversions
        -> better ranking and product mappings
        -> better merchant economics
        -> more catalog/feed partners
        -> better coverage and exact-match evidence
        -> better user results
```

Do not accumulate copyrighted raw video as the moat. Prefer derived evidence, product mappings, corrections, and commercial outcomes.

## Visual-intent analytics

A future recurring-revenue product can expose aggregate demand hidden inside video/content:
- objects/categories viewers select,
- selection rate by content/timestamp,
- products with repeated unresolved demand,
- exact/likely/similar distribution,
- merchant outbound rate,
- conversion where observable,
- revenue per intent event.

This can be sold even when the publisher or retailer controls the final commerce destination.

## Alpha monetization and cost controls

Closed alpha first.

Recommended controls:
- small invite-only cohort,
- per-user weekly/monthly Scoop-event limit,
- server-side usage counter,
- global monthly inference/provider budget ceiling,
- graceful limit-reached state,
- no unlimited plan,
- BYOK path for heavy technical users later.

Do not price on model tokens. Price or quota on Scoop intent/analysis events.

Measure before increasing spend:
- inference cost per event,
- total cost per completed Scoop,
- useful-result rate,
- repeat-use rate,
- merchant outbound CTR,
- observable conversion rate,
- revenue per intent event.

## Commercial experiments in order

### Experiment 1 — prove consumer commerce

Goal: first tracked affiliate click and first attributable commission.

Use Awin first; add a second commercial adapter only when useful.

### Experiment 2 — prove willingness to pay for infrastructure

Sell one bounded B2B pilot.

Possible pilot:
- one catalog,
- defined number of videos/assets or API events,
- fixed duration,
- measured visual selections,
- clear exact/likely/similar behavior,
- analytics summary.

Do not require a full enterprise platform to run the pilot.

### Experiment 3 — developer/BYOK

Expose the existing engine through a controlled developer plan once the internal API is stable enough to support external users.

Test willingness to pay before building extensive developer tooling.

### Experiment 4 — partner/network distribution

Only after measurable commerce value:
- evaluate Awin Technology Partner,
- video-player/CMS integrations,
- larger publisher/retailer partnerships,
- direct merchant catalog integrations.

## Do not

- Do not rely on affiliate commissions as the only route to $10k MRR.
- Do not assume brands will pay merely because Scoop can add interactivity to a video.
- Do not sell a feature that customers can reproduce in an afternoon as though it is a moat.
- Do not promise universal exact matching.
- Do not allow merchant payouts to affect EXACT / LIKELY / SIMILAR classification.
- Do not integrate every affiliate network before one route proves value.
- Do not build separate commerce logic for every network.
- Do not pay for broad infrastructure before measured usage requires it.
- Do not build enterprise dashboards before a customer asks for them.
- Do not confuse traffic acquisition value with software value.
- Do not require consumer mass adoption before testing B2B revenue.

## Decision rule

When choosing commercial work, prefer tasks that improve at least two of these simultaneously:

1. user utility,
2. product-resolution quality,
3. merchant/catalog coverage,
4. proprietary evidence/outcome data,
5. recurring revenue potential,
6. provider/platform independence.

Avoid work that creates revenue only by weakening trust or adding dependence on a single platform, merchant, affiliate network, or model provider.

## Immediate commercial sequence

1. Finish the reliability gate for the core Scoop interaction.
2. Launch a tightly capped closed alpha.
3. Join Awin as a publisher and verify a tracked affiliate destination.
4. Record the first complete `intent -> merchant click -> outcome` event.
5. Measure actual cost per Scoop.
6. Add only one additional affiliate/commercial adapter if Awin coverage is insufficient.
7. Use alpha evidence to approach one small B2B catalog/video customer for a paid pilot.
8. Test a BYOK developer tier only after the internal API is stable.
9. Pursue Awin Technology Partner or similar distribution only after Scoop can demonstrate measurable value.
10. Continuously build the product/intent/outcome graph so the business becomes progressively harder to clone.
